# What if the 2008 market crash never happened? — Beat sheet (Step A)

**Format:** 1080 × 1920 vertical · 30 fps · 62.5 s total (1,875 frames)
**Structure:** 13 beats × 4.5 s = 58.5 s, then a 4 s end card.
**Always on screen:** title at top ("What if the 2008 market crash never happened?"), a caption, and a small stats panel.
**Labels:** gold **REAL HISTORY** tag on every sourced number · teal **WHAT IF** tag on every imagined outcome (always described in words, never with invented numbers).
**Music:** original code-generated ukulele loop, 120 BPM, C–G–Am–F. During hardship facts it drops to soft plucks with no joke sounds.
Sources for every number are in [`SOURCES.md`](SOURCES.md) (S1, S2, … below).

## The alternate-timeline assumption

**One change:** around 2005, regulators require every mortgage lender to check that borrowers can repay from their income. In real life, the Fed adopted that rule in July 2008, after the boom was over (S5).

**What follows from that, in this one plausible timeline:**
- Fewer risky loans get made.
- The housing boom cools slowly instead of collapsing.
- There's no sudden wave of defaults, so the panic that brought down Lehman Brothers doesn't spread.

**Held the same as real history:** everything else, including the Fed's goals, global trade, population growth, and the 2020 pandemic.

**What we do NOT assume:** a perfect economy. A milder slowdown could still happen, stocks don't rise forever, nobody gets automatically rich, and new risks can still build up. Economists genuinely disagree about how this would have played out, so every WHAT IF outcome is phrased as "might" or "could" and given no number.

## Notes for review

- **Where the city shows hardship:** beats 6, 7, 8, 10 and 13 cover real hardship. It appears only in captions and stats. The city shows the WHAT IF version, and the jokes only involve objects (spaghetti, piggy banks, price tags, the coaster).
- **Correction to a common claim:** the April 2020 job loss is often quoted as "22 million." The official BLS figure for that month is **20.5 million**, so the video uses 20.5 million.
- **Rounded figures:**
  - "~79%" in beat 9 is 478K ÷ 2.27M, rounded.
  - Beat 11 compares one month (July 2007) with a full-year figure (2024). Both come from Census, and the caption labels each one.
