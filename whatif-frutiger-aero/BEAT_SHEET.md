# What if the world went full Frutiger Aero? — Beat sheet (Step A)

**Format:** 1080 × 1920 · 30 fps · 62.5 s (13 beats × 4.5 s + a 4 s end card), same pipeline as the first two videos.
**Premise:** Frutiger Aero is the glossy mid-2000s tech look: bubbles, clean water, bright grass, glass panels, blue skies, fish, lens flares, Windows Vista. It sold a future where technology and nature live together. **What if that future actually showed up?**
**Always on screen:** the title "What if the world went full Frutiger Aero?", a caption with a REAL fact, a stats panel with one **REAL** or **NAPKIN MATH** row and one **WHAT IF** row, and a "WHAT IF CITY · imagined" badge.

## The look (this is the fun part)
The city itself turns Frutiger Aero as the video goes on:
- **Glass and gloss:** buildings become translucent blue-green glass with highlights; every surface gets that "Web 2.0" shine.
- **Nature plus tech:** grass runs up onto rooftops, water channels wind between buildings, koi swim in them, solar leaves and wind flowers spin.
- **Floating bubbles** drift everywhere, plus the occasional lens flare.
- **Palette:** sky blue, aqua, lime green, white, with glossy orange accents.
- **Sound:** an original "2000s startup chime" soundtrack written in code: soft electric piano chords, a bubbly synth arpeggio, a warm pad and gentle "bloop" effects. Think the mood of an old OS boot-up sound, but it's ours.

## The what-if assumption
One change: the future the 2004–2013 tech ads promised actually arrives, town by town. Four themes carry the story: renewable energy, community programs, local innovation and organic pride. Every WHAT IF outcome is described in words; every number on screen is a REAL fact (or clearly labeled NAPKIN MATH on real inputs).

## Napkin-math assumption
- Beat 10: "700 GW ≈ 700 large power plants" uses 1 GW as a typical large plant (roughly one big nuclear or coal unit). It's a rough comparison, labeled as such.

## Producer notes
- **Nothing is sold as a prediction.** Renewables, libraries, repair cafés, organic sales and gardens are all real and growing; the WHAT IF only imagines them spread everywhere and dressed in gloss.
- **The twist is honest and still wholesome:** the gadget era left a real e-waste problem, and the aesthetic itself faded. The takeaway turns that into pride: the glossy future is maintained, not promised.
- **Brand safety:** "Windows Vista" and "TikTok" appear only as plain-text facts, no logos.
