# Sources and licences — “What if it rained in Utah for 30 days?”

This is a **fictional scenario**: the 30 days of rain, the runoff, the flooding, the rising lakes and the day-by-day timeline are imagined, and every animated consequence on screen is labelled “imagined scenario”. No invented rainfall or lake-rise figure is presented as a measurement or prediction; the scenario uses qualitative gauges (runoff, ground saturation, flood pressure) instead.
**Utah's geography is real**: the rivers, basins, drainage divide, terrain and places are drawn from open data, and every real-world number in a caption or stats panel traces to a row in **Facts** below.

How these were checked: each figure was cross-checked against the agency or publication below through web search. This machine's network cannot open nps.gov directly, so NPS quotes come from search excerpts of the NPS pages. **Open each link once before publishing.** Where sources disagree, the caption uses the rounder, more conservative figure and says “about”.

| Elevation | Terrain relief (2× vertical), the lake's uneven growth over low ground, canyon dioramas | [Terrain Tiles on AWS](https://registry.opendata.aws/terrain-tiles/), from USGS 3DEP / SRTM / NOAA ETOPO1 | Open data with attribution (below) |

**Elevation attribution:** United States 3DEP (formerly NED) and SRTM terrain data courtesy of the U.S. Geological Survey · Global ETOPO1 terrain data, U.S. National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration · tiles by Mapzen.

**Projection:** NAD83 / UTM zone 12N (EPSG:26912), the projection Utah's state GIS (UGRC) uses for statewide data; the globe is an orthographic view.

**Imagined layers (marked “imagined scenario” in every legend):** rainfall intensity, runoff arrows, temporary greenery, new shorelines, flooded flats and valley floors. The new shoreline is computed from the real terrain (water fills the lowest ground first), but its extent is illustrative, not a prediction.
